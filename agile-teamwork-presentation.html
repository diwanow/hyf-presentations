Part 01 · Fundamentals

The Agile Manifesto

Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
Working software over comprehensive documentation
Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
Responding to change over following a plan
That is, while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more.
Learn more: The 12 Agile Principles
Part 01 · Fundamentals

The five values

Commitment
Team members dedicate themselves to achieving the sprint goals and supporting each other.
Focus
Keep attention on the sprint goal and the work that matters most right now.
Openness
Share what’s working, what isn’t, and ask for help without fear.
Respect
Value each team member’s knowledge, skills, and effort.
Courage
Make tough decisions, give honest feedback, and admit mistakes.
Together
These values make teamwork possible and sustainable.
Part 01 · Fundamentals

The Scrum events

1
Sprint Planning — Team agrees on what to build this sprint
2
Daily Standup — Quick sync on progress, blockers, and plans
3
Sprint Review — Show what we built, get feedback
4

These events create the structure for your work. Make them yours.

Part 02 · During each sprint

How to structure 50 minutes

1
5 minutes — Sprint planning: agree on what to build
2
Sync as needed — Daily scrum: your choice how often and how
3
30 minutes — Build: code, test, discuss, make it real
4
10 minutes total — Demos: 5 minutes per team (2 min demo + 3 min Q&A)
5
5 minutes — Retrospective: what went well, what to improve next sprint
Part 02 · During each sprint

Retrospective idea

Quickly rate 1–5:
Team work
Clarity
Quality
Outcome
Out of the lowest scoring, brainstorm possible actions and select 1 to focus on next sprint.
Part 02 · Team structure

Responsibilities, not silos

1 Product Owner
Ensures direction and validates work from a user perspective. Can still write code. Focused on: what should we build?
Team co-owns Quality
Everyone contributes to testing and quality. Focused on: does it work and is it good?
UX (Choose your model)
Option A: Product Owner takes on UX
Option B: Co-own UX as a team
Decide what works best for your group.
Taking a responsibility doesn’t stop you from coding. Everyone contributes.
🔄 Switch roles each sprint so everyone experiences different responsibilities.

Some takeaways

My observations

Working alone is convenient, but often limiting
Communication is key — enables teamwork, stress reduces it
Planning is hard — but creating clarity is too important
Retrospectives are easy to neglect — they are your way to gain control; agree on and take action
Sometimes we need to cut corners — long-term it bites (tech debt)
Work small and integrate early - not doing it is risky and costly
You succeed and fail as a team
Working overtime is a slippery slope
