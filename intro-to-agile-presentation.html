Lost at Sea

Your yacht catches fire in the Atlantic. The crew is gone, the boat is sinking, and your location is unclear.

You have a lifeboat, matches, and 15 saved items. Rank every item from 1 (most important) to 15 (least important) for survival while waiting for rescue.

Lost at Sea · individual · 10 minutes

Make your own ranking

Work individually

Read the scenario and rank all 15 items. Give each a different position.

Keep your answers

Write down your ranking so you can compare it with your team's later.

Lost at Sea · team · 30 minutes

Agree as a team

After the individual ranking, work in teams of 3–4 to create one team ranking.

For this exercise, everyone must agree. Listen to each other, explain your choices, and write down the final order.

Lost at Sea · debrief · 25 minutes

What did you notice?

The facilitator intentionally changed the time limit to prompt reflection.

  • How did your team reach decisions? Was everyone heard?
  • What changed when the time limit changed? Who was heard?
  • How did your team ranking compare with individual rankings?
  • What would you do differently next time?

Agile connection: Teams communicate, make decisions and adapt when circumstances change.

Ways of working

Waterfall and Agile

Waterfall

Plan, design, build, test and deliver in largely sequential stages. Feedback often comes later.

Agile

Build in small steps, get feedback early and often, and adapt as you learn.

Sequential work can suit stable requirements, but late feedback can mean costly rework. Agile revisits the plan as we learn.

The Agile Manifesto

People and working software

Individuals and interactions over processes and tools

Talk through a blocker together, even when it is already recorded on the board.

Working software over comprehensive documentation

Show a working feature early, while keeping the documentation people need.

The items on the right have value; we value those on the left more.

The Agile Manifesto

Collaboration and change

Customer collaboration over contract negotiation

Ask the customer for feedback during development, not only when agreeing the contract.

Responding to change over following a plan

Reorder the plan when feedback reveals a more urgent need.

Contracts and plans still matter. Read the Manifesto ↗

A practical framework

Scrum basics

Agile is a mindset. Scrum is one framework for putting it into practice.

Sprint

A fixed period of one month or less, chosen by the team, to work toward a goal and finish something useful.

Product backlog

An ordered list of work that may be needed for the product.

Sprint backlog

The work selected for the current sprint and the plan to deliver it.

Read the official Scrum Guide ↗

Scrum · values

Five values for teamwork

Commitment

Work toward the team's goals and support each other.

Focus

Pay attention to the sprint goal and the work that matters now.

Openness

Share progress and challenges, and ask for help.

Respect

Value each person's skills, knowledge and contribution.

Courage

Raise concerns, give honest feedback and tackle difficult problems.

Scrum · team

One team, three accountabilities

Scrum teams organise their own work and share responsibility for a useful result.

Product Owner

Helps maximise product value and is accountable for ordering the product backlog.

Scrum Master

Helps the team understand Scrum, improve how it works and address obstacles.

Developers

Plan and create the useful result. Their skills can include design, testing and coding.

Scrum · events

How a sprint unfolds

The sprint is the fixed period that contains these events:

Sprint Planning

Agree on a sprint goal and plan the work to achieve it.

Daily Scrum

Developers check progress toward the goal and adjust their plan each day.

Sprint Review

Inspect the result with stakeholders and discuss what to do next.

Sprint Retrospective

Reflect on how the team worked and plan improvements.

Demo · board flow

From idea to done

  • Product backlog: all potential work, ordered by priority.
  • Sprint backlog: selected work and the plan for this sprint; other work stays in the product backlog.
  • In progress: work underway. Discuss blockers or dependencies with the team.
  • Done: the team has checked its agreed completion criteria, such as a confirmed booking.
Board setup · 10 minutes

Plan a birthday party

In class, sign in or create a free account at Trello. Create a board with Product backlog, Sprint backlog, In progress, and Done lists.

Add these seven cards to the product backlog. They are not yet in priority order:

  • Buy decorations
  • Order the birthday cake
  • Send out invitations
  • Book the venue
  • Choose and buy party snacks
  • Plan the playlist / music
  • Plan the date
Prioritisation

Now / Next / Later

When there is lots to do, decide what matters first.

Now

Urgent work or decisions that unblock other tasks.

Next

Important work to do soon, after its dependencies.

Later

Useful ideas that can wait.

Consider deadlines, dependencies and value. Several tasks can be Now; explain your choices together.

After setup · pairs · 10 minutes

What's first?

  1. With a partner, decide which birthday tasks are Now, Next or Later. Discuss your reasons.
  2. Order your product backlog: Now at the top, then Next, then Later.
  3. Move all Now tasks into your sprint backlog.

Then share and compare your reasons as a group (3 more minutes).
