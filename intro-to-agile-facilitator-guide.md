# Intro to Agile: facilitator guide

**Private facilitator notes:** Do not project or distribute this guide. It contains the Lost at Sea surprise. Print or copy only the item sheet below for learners; it has no answer key or timing twist.

## Before class

- Open the presentation locally, test the projector, navigation, Lost at Sea site and Trello. Keep this guide available offline and print the item sheet for each learner and team. Bring pens, paper, sticky notes or small cards, and a timer.
- Ask participants to prepare a free Trello account before class if possible; they can also sign up in class. Check device, network and email verification access. Have pairs share a device or use a paper board if signup, access or the network fails. Allow extra time for account issues.
- Arrange teams of 3-4. For the rocket demonstration, recruit two learner volunteers: one customer and one artist. Prepare a way to hide the first drawing from the customer, then show the second drawing as it develops.
- If expert comparison is important, save or print the answer checker's verified expert ranking before class. The item sheet below deliberately contains no answers. Without a verified key, compare individual and team reasoning and do not invent scores or a rescue probability.

## Run of show

Times are sequential, not overlapping. Allow **50 actual minutes** for Lost at Sea: 10 individual + 15 team + 25 debrief. This extends the source's 10-minute debrief to leave room for checking results and discussion. Allow transitions and any extra setup separately; do not reveal the actual team timing before the exercise.

1. **Lost at Sea, individual (10 min):** Explain the scenario on the slide. Everyone ranks all 15 from 1 to 15, with no ties, and keeps their individual list. Use the site, or distribute the item sheet and have them write ranks beside each item.
2. **Lost at Sea, team (15 min actual):** Form groups of 3-4. Announce **30 minutes after the individual stage**, and require one unanimous team order for this exercise. Start a timer. At exactly 10 minutes, interrupt: "Stop! The fire has become more violent. You have only 5 more minutes to agree on your final ranking. You need to agree now or the team has failed the exercise. Continue!" Give exactly 5 more minutes. Observe participation and decision-making; do not reveal the interruption in advance.
3. **Debrief and reveal (25 min):** Allow about 10-15 minutes to enter and compare team results with the online checker or a verified expert ranking, and 10-15 minutes for discussion. State plainly that you deliberately announced 30 minutes and that the change to 15 minutes was planned to prompt reflection. Acknowledge learners' feelings, including any frustration, and invite them to opt out of sharing. Ask what changed under pressure, whose views were heard, how decisions were made, and how the team ranking differed from individual rankings. A group can compare orders without an expert score if the checker or verified ranking is unavailable. Connect the experience to Agile: unexpected change requires communication, reassessment and adaptation. Unanimity is this exercise's rule, not a universal Scrum requirement; teams may use other agreed decision methods.
4. **Project management, approaches and values:** Allow time for discussion. Explain that late feedback may cause rework, while a sequential approach can suit stable requirements. For the values, the items on the right still matter; the left are valued more.
5. **Rocket demonstration (10 min):** The facilitator guides two learner volunteers: a customer and an artist. Use the same pair for both drawings. Spend 3 minutes on the concealed Waterfall drawing and end feedback, 4 minutes redrawing incrementally with customer feedback, and 3 minutes comparing results. The customer describes their desired rocket; the artist draws. The point is early feedback, not artistic skill. Link the short feedback cycles to Scrum's sprint inspection and adaptation.
6. **Scrum and Trello demo:** Allow about 20-25 minutes for Scrum and questions, plus time for the board demo. Explain sprint and backlogs; introduce the five values, the Product Owner, Scrum Master and Developers, and the events within a sprint. The team organises its own work; the Scrum Master is not the team's boss. Use the rocket exercise to contrast a review of the result with a retrospective about how people worked. Explain agreed completion criteria and demonstrate one card moving through the four lists; remaining ideas stay in the product backlog. This simplified teaching board illustrates flow, not all of Scrum. A blocked card can stay in progress with a visible note while the team discusses the dependency.
7. **Birthday board setup (10 min):** In class, participants sign in or register at Trello, create a board, add the four lists and enter the seven intentionally unordered cards from the slide. If accounts take too long, use the paper alternative below and do not sacrifice the later discussion.
8. **Prioritisation (10 min), then group sharing (3 additional min):** Pairs consider deadlines, dependencies and value, group their cards Now/Next/Later, order them, and move Now cards to the sprint backlog. Multiple cards can be Now; for this exercise assume they fit the sprint. Ask pairs to explain differences and tradeoffs. In real work the team also checks capacity and a sprint goal.
9. **Wrap-up:** Link prioritising together to the Lost at Sea discussion and the rocket's feedback loop: make work visible, choose, inspect and adjust. Mention that Agile Teamwork is a separate session on another day; its activities are not part of this session.

The named exercises and debrief occupy 83 minutes in total, before explanation, demo, setup delays, transitions or breaks. Allow extra time for the expanded Scrum section and use the session buffer as needed to keep lunch around 14:00 and finish by 16:00.

## Offline procedures

**Lost at Sea:** Read the slide's scenario aloud. Give everyone a copy of the item sheet and a pen; lifeboat and matches are already available and are not ranked. Learners number every item uniquely, then teams make a separate numbered 1-15 list. Run the same announced and actual timing above. Use the debrief even if the answer checker is unavailable; do not claim a computed score without its verified key.

**Trello:** Draw four columns on paper or a whiteboard: Product backlog, Sprint backlog, In progress, Done. Write each birthday task on a separate sticky note or paper card. Place all seven in Product backlog in their original unordered sequence. Sort and move cards physically as in the slides. For the demo, move one sample card across each column and state the agreed completion criterion. If notes are unavailable, write cards on paper and cross out and rewrite them as they move.
